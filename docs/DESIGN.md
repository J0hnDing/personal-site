# Portfolio design

## Reference study

Reviewed the live references in a browser before implementation, including their opening compositions and scroll states. These were studies in visual behavior, not sources for copied assets, copy, or layouts.

- [Dennis Snellenberg](https://dennissnellenberg.com/): multilingual greeting, generous scale, a name that extends beyond the frame, and continuity between large forms and navigation.
- [Ansyn](https://ansyn.me/): a brief greeting into a dispersed typographic field, restrained metadata, and a personal rather than corporate voice.
- [Khanh Nguyen](https://khanhnguyen.design/): exceptionally large editorial typography, frame edges as a compositional tool, and substantial changes in composition across scrolling chapters.
- [Lama Lama](https://lamalama.com/): a coherent visual world, confident contrasts in scale, compact navigation, and visual transitions that carry the identity through the page.
- [JIEJOE](https://www.jiejoe.com/): expressive oversized type, geometric interaction, and deliberate movement between compositions.
- [Antoine Wodniack](https://wodniack.dev/): a graphic system that extends through scrolling, strong color, and typography acting as image.

Five unboxed navigation words link to Home, Projects, Gallery, Thoughts, and Contact. About is reached through the quick introduction's “More about me” link. The full project index, gallery, thoughts, and contact content follow on the same page; individual project and thought details remain separate routes.

## Home Gallery tiling — October 2, 2026

October 3 original caching: Home queues every original after the initial document assets and fonts are ready, including while the intro/hero is displayed. One idle-scheduled request runs at a time with a [low fetch priority hint](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/RequestInit#priority); visible Gallery photos move ahead in the queue. Leaving Home, hiding the tab, or leaving the page cancels scheduled work and aborts unneeded downloads. Opening an uncached photo interrupts unrelated speculative work and shares any existing request for that photo. Cache Storage retains compressed originals between visits, with source size/mtime versioned URLs and removal of obsolete entries. Storage restrictions use a bounded 32 MiB compressed-memory fallback. Blob URLs are created only for displayed photos, pinned while displayed, and trimmed to six sources/32 MiB when unused; at most two recently decoded images are retained. Home's visible photos and both enlargement viewers use cached originals. The spinner stays until an uncached original arrives and decodes; a ready decoded original can reopen immediately. The entire collection is not decoded in the background. Fetch priority is a browser hint, and connection speed/cache availability still determine when originals become ready.

Clicking a Home Gallery photograph expands it from its measured canvas rectangle into a large centered image, with responsive margins of 24–80px. A 420ms transform animation preserves its position and aspect ratio through the expansion; dismissal returns it to the source in 300ms. The rest of the page blurs, loses color, and dims beneath a translucent grey backdrop. The preview stays visible beneath a centered rotating ring while the original loads and decodes, then the full-resolution image fades in. The canvas starts with 1,800px WebP previews and upgrades on-screen photographs to their original resolution as the background cache becomes ready. The viewer shares that original and loads it on demand if it is not yet available. Browser-readable originals are copied unchanged, while TIFF sources become lossless PNGs without resizing. Enter or Space also opens a focused photo. Escape, the close button, or the backdrop dismisses the viewer and restores focus; dragging continues to pan. Buffered off-screen photos are excluded from keyboard tab order, and the canvas clips overflow so focusing a photo cannot scroll its geometry independently of the camera. The custom cursor follows native dialogs into their top layer and keeps its last pointer position, so it remains visible above the viewer. Reduced motion bypasses expansion, dismissal, crossfade, and spinner rotation.

The Home Gallery uses a seeded rectangular tiling rather than an attachment tree. Generation starts with the photographs: each image's native aspect ratio and desired display area determine its exact rectangle, then compact blank rectangles complete its reserved region. Photos fill their entire cells, preserving the full image without cropping or internal gutters. A jittered coordinate lattice keeps every rectangle's width and height at least 112px on desktop or 89.6px below 700px viewport width. Each finite chunk reserves rectangles crossing every internal coordinate line, plus rectangles crossing its right and bottom boundaries; neighboring chunks share those crossings. Remaining squares are filled completely, then full-side merges vary the proportions. Shared blank edges shift together to replace most four-way crosses with offset T junctions; photo dimensions stay fixed. Compact shape limits prevent skinny filler rectangles. Varied photo positions, bridge placement, rectangle choices, merges, and coordinate spacing disguise the scaffold. The geometry has no uncovered pockets, overlapping rectangles, or infinite straight seams. A conservative bound for any continuous collinear edge is 38.38 times the minimum dimension.

Chunks generate directly around the viewport with a 360px buffer, including distant and negative coordinates. There is no world-size or rectangle-count cap; geometry stays fixed when exploring or returning. Visited chunks remain cached, so memory grows with exploration, and coordinates remain subject to JavaScript numeric precision. Real photographs have a target square-equivalent size of 482px on desktop or 385px on mobile, varying by only six percent in either direction while preserving aspect ratios. This doubles their area again compared with the 341px/272px revision. Placement scores randomly positioned photo rectangles for crowding and average/maximum empty space at sampled points, including chunk edges. A random score contribution and random corner orientations preserve irregular spacing and alignment; there are no assigned quarters or fixed targets. Each chunk aims for four photos and can retain a feasible three-photo partition when packing or seam constraints require it. Photo selection and placement constrain the partition before any filler is generated, and every reservation must leave the remaining seams breakable. Nine shuffled spatial pools keep each photo unique within a chunk and separate repeats by roughly 3,000 world pixels on desktop; selection rotates within each pool so the full collection remains represented. A visible-photo guard suppresses additional copies even in unusually wide or zoomed-out views, prioritizing on-screen photos over buffered ones. Existing panning, zoom, title animation, and reduced-motion behavior remain intact. The standalone gallery-tree experiment is separate and retains its existing algorithm.

Photo placement also adds a soft penalty proportional to the square of each shared photo-edge length. Longer touching segments are less attractive; corner-only contact and separated rectangles have zero cost. Coverage, crowding, and random score contributions can still outweigh this penalty. Neighbor plans are resolved in two alternating sets so photo contacts across chunk boundaries are considered without making the result depend on exploration order.

Scroll-to-zoom ranges from 50% to 200% on desktop and phones, keeping the photograph under the pointer fixed while zooming. The visible-photo guard also applies at the wider 50% view.
